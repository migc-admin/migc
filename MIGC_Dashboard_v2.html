How the Sandbagger Score Works

What is Sandbagging?

In golf, "sandbagging" means intentionally playing worse than your ability — or allowing your handicap to inflate — to gain a competitive advantage in net events. Our score flags players whose handicap patterns look statistically unusual based on index history alone.

The Four Signals (scored 0–100 total)

  • 🔴 Recent Trend (up to 35 pts) — Is the handicap rising lately? Compares the avg of the last 10 GHIN entries vs. the 10 before that. Rising index = more points.
  • 🟡 Gap From Personal Best (up to 35 pts) — How far above their all-time low is the player's current HCI? A 5-stroke gap scores more than a 1-stroke gap.
  • 🔵 Consistency Rating (up to 20 pts) — Highly erratic handicaps can indicate manipulation. A very stable, flat history scores zero here.
  • ⚪ Overall Regression (up to 10 pts) — Is the current HCI higher than when the player first joined? Only fires for net regressions over full history.

Risk Tiers

HIGH Score ≥ 55 — Pattern is statistically notable. Worth monitoring.
MEDIUM Score 30–54 — Some signals present. Watch over time.
LOW Score < 30 — No significant patterns detected.

Worked Example — Max O'Brien (Score: 57.6 · High)

Started: 15.3 HCI  |  Current: 23.8 HCI  |  Rounds: 61

🔴 Recent Trend: Index has been climbing (+0.76 avg) → 7.6 pts
🟡 Gap from Best: 8.5 strokes above personal best (15.3) → 35.0 pts
🔵 Consistency: Moderate swing (std dev 1.67) → 5.0 pts
⚪ Regression: Net +8.5 from starting index → 10.0 pts

Total: 57.6 · HIGH RISK — Index has never been lower than the starting point. Steady upward drift across 3 years of history.

Important Disclaimer

A high score is a statistical flag, not an accusation. Injury, reduced play frequency, switching to harder courses, or natural decline can all cause legitimate handicap increases. This tool uses only GHIN index history — no round-level data, course ratings, or slope information is available. Use as a conversation starter, not a conclusion.
